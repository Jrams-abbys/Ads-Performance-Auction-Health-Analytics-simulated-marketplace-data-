Ads Performance & Auction Health Analytics
SQL (SQLite) · Python · Excel · Business Analytics

An end-to-end portfolio project analysing the advertising business of a simulated food-delivery marketplace. The analysis covers weekly ad KPIs, advertiser and category performance, inventory utilisation, auction health, advertiser retention, revenue opportunity sizing, and an ad-format A/B test readout.
Data disclosure: All data is synthetic. The project simulates a 1% sample of marketplace ad logs for January–September 2026, with INR-denominated metrics. The data is generated by generate_data.py using a fixed random seed. It is not Swiggy data and is not affiliated with Swiggy. The generator deliberately sets key marketplace mechanics and the A/B treatment effect; the project demonstrates the analytical workflow, not real-world Swiggy results.

Business questions
- How are ad revenue, CPM, CTR, fill rate, advertiser count, and ROAS changing over time?
- Which advertiser categories deliver the strongest ROAS, and which represent optimisation opportunities?
- Which ad placements are supply-constrained, and where is inventory under-utilised?
- How competitive are auctions across placements, based on bidder counts and single-bidder share?
- Which advertiser cohorts continue spending after signup, and where is retention weakest?
- What is the illustrative revenue impact of a premium placement or improved inventory sell-through?
- Does a new ad format improve CTR, and are downstream conversion guardrails sufficiently powered?
Dataset and schema
The generator creates a synthetic 1% sample-scale dataset covering 130 advertisers, 8 advertiser categories, and approximately 2.4 million impressions from January through September 2026.
Table	What it contains
advertisers	Advertiser, category, signup date, and monthly budget
slots	Ad placements and floor CPMs
inventory	Daily available and filled impressions by placement
impressions	Impression timestamp, advertiser, placement, CPM paid, bidder count, and experiment variant
clicks	Clicks linked to impressions
orders	Attributed orders and order value linked to clicks

Key metric definitions
- Ad revenue (platform): SUM(cpm_paid) / 1,000.
- CTR: clicks divided by impressions.
- CPM: platform ad revenue per 1,000 impressions.
- Fill rate: filled impressions divided by available impressions.
- ROAS: attributed order value divided by platform ad spend.
- Advertiser retention: share of a signup cohort that is still spending at the specified day milestone.
The project distinguishes platform ad revenue from advertiser-attributed order value; they are not interchangeable.
Selected findings from the simulated data
1. Overall inventory utilisation increased: weekly fill rate rose from 39.2% in the week beginning 5 January 2026 to 91.2% in the week beginning 21 September 2026.
2. Placement utilisation diverged by September: monthly fill rate was 99.9% for search_top, 100.0% for restaurant_listing, and 65.7% for home_banner. Across the full observation period, 32.4% of home_banner auctions had a single bidder, suggesting that demand generation may be a more useful lever to test there than simply increasing the floor price.
3. Category ROAS varied substantially: QSR Chains recorded 4.63x ROAS, compared with 0.78x for Personal Care. Snacks & FMCG represented 24.0% of spend at 1.46x ROAS, making it a sizeable category to investigate for performance improvement.
4. Retention differed by category: Local Restaurants had approximately 3.98x ROAS, but only 18.8% of the measured cohort was still spending at day 90 (3 of 16 advertisers). The cohort is small, so this is directional rather than conclusive.
5. Advertiser spend was concentrated: the top 10 advertisers accounted for approximately 39.9% of total spend.
6. Scenario-based opportunity sizing: the workbook's base-case assumptions estimate approximately 3.3% potential ad-revenue uplift from a premium search_top placement and improved home_banner sell-through. This is a scenario, not a measured outcome; the inputs are explicit assumptions.
7. Simulated A/B readout: the dish-image carousel treatment showed a 25.4% relative CTR lift (95% CI: 14.9% to 35.9%, p < 0.001), with CPM broadly unchanged. The click-to-order guardrail was underpowered: the experiment had only 774 clicks in control and 964 in treatment, versus approximately 9,008 clicks per arm needed to detect a 15% relative decline at 80% power. The readout therefore recommends a monitored rollout or retaining a holdout rather than treating the guardrail as proven safe.
Methodology and limitations
- The dataset is generated synthetically with a deterministic seed. It does not represent actual Swiggy users, advertisers, transactions, auctions, or business performance.
- The A/B treatment effect is deliberately encoded in the generator (+16% true CTR probability multiplier and -4% click-to-order probability multiplier). The observed experiment statistics are outputs from that simulation, not evidence from a live experiment.
- Opportunity-sizing inputs (for example, premium inventory share, premium fill, CPM uplift, discount, and target fill rate) are judgement-based scenario assumptions. They should be validated through pilots and advertiser demand/price-elasticity tests.
- Retention cuts have small cohorts (category cohort sizes range from 5 to 16 advertisers in the reported view), so interpret them directionally.
- The SQL is written for SQLite. The analytical concepts are transferable, but date functions and some syntax may need adjustment for MySQL or PostgreSQL.
Run the project locally
Requirements: Python, plus numpy, pandas, and openpyxl.
pip install numpy pandas openpyxl
python generate_data.py
python run_analysis.py
python build_workbook.py
This creates ads.db locally, runs the SQL in queries.sql, exports the analysis outputs to results/, and builds Ads_Analytics_Workbook.xlsx.

You can inspect ads.db using DB Browser for SQLite.

Repository note: ads.db is generated locally and is not required in GitHub. Keep it out of version control; the generator recreates it.
